Cumulative insurance cost above the CPI-projected trajectory: ~$829,500 (1990–2025)

Had Riveredge's insurance costs simply grown at the rate of general inflation (CPI), the building would have paid an estimated $1.09 million in insurance over 36 years. Actual insurance paid: $1.92 million. The difference — approximately $829,500 — is the amount insurance costs have outpaced inflation, or roughly $9.76 per share over 36 years ($0.27/share/year on average). Unlike real estate taxes, insurance shows no comparable pattern of successful appeals bringing costs back toward the inflation trend.

2002: +31% year-over-year, the steepest single-year jump on record
2004: +29% year-over-year, the second-steepest jump
2022–2025: four years account for $234,893 (28%) of the entire 36-year gap
⚠   Insurance has never tracked below the CPI trajectory. From 1991 onward, every single year's actual insurance cost exceeded what inflation alone would have produced from the 1990 baseline — the opposite pattern from real estate taxes over the same period. The gap has also been accelerating: the four years 2022–2025 alone contributed 28% of the entire 36-year cumulative overage, with 2024 and 2025 posting back-to-back double-digit percentage jumps ($86,587 → $99,094 → $115,384).
